# Materials and hardware — sizing data

Reference values for choosing panels and sizing hardware. Thicknesses and formats vary by supplier:
**always confirm what's actually available** before the cutting diagram.

## Panels — common thicknesses (mm)

| Panel | Common thicknesses (mm) | Typical use |
|-------|-------------------------|-------------|
| MDF | 3, 6, 9, 12, 16, 18, 19, 22, 25, 30 | Painted fronts, moldings, backs, jigs. Smooth surface, ideal for paint. Heavy, weak end grain, fine dust is harmful. |
| Plywood | 5, 8, 10, 12, 15, 18, 21, 25 | Structure, backs, drawers, stressed parts. Excellent strength-to-weight; nice edge (multi-ply). |
| Melamine-faced board (MFC) | 8, 16, 18, 19, 25 | Carcasses, shelves, interiors. Ready to use, economical; edges need banding; holds screws poorly in the edge. |
| Raw particleboard / chipboard | 8, 16, 18, 19, 25, 38 | Backing, substrate, surfaces to be covered. Cheap, low moisture resistance. |
| OSB | 9, 12, 15, 18, 22 | Structure, raw look, backs. Economical, strong visual texture. |
| Solid wood | varies (18–40 typical for furniture) | Tops, fronts, fine structures. Moves with humidity (manage wood movement). |

**Recommended default thicknesses**: carcasses and shelves **18 mm**; backs **6–8 mm** (grooved) or
8–12 mm (surface-mounted); painted fronts **18–19 mm** MDF; loaded shelves **18 mm** solid/plywood or
doubled up.

## Panels — standard formats (mm)

Indicative; verify with the supplier (and decor direction for melamine).

| Panel | Common formats (mm) |
|-------|---------------------|
| Melamine / particleboard | 2800 × 2070, 3050 × 2070, sometimes 2620 × 2070; half-sheets 2070 × 1400 |
| MDF | 2800 × 2070, 3050 × 1220, 2440 × 1220 |
| Plywood | 2500 × 1250, 3100 × 1530, 2440 × 1220 |
| OSB | 2500 × 1250, 2440 × 1220 |

The cutting diagram must start from the format **actually** bought, kerf deducted (3–4 mm).

## Choosing the species (solid wood)

- **Softwoods (pine, fir, spruce)**: cheap, light, easy to work; soft (dent), knots, noticeable
  movement. Everyday furniture, structures, raw look.
- **Beech**: hard, even, good for joints and worktops; yellows, moves with humidity.
- **Oak**: strong, durable, beautiful grain; heavy, tannin (stains with iron/moisture), high price.
- **Ash**: hard yet springy, light-colored, beautiful figure; a good compromise for stressed parts.
- **Walnut**: premium, dark, stable; expensive, reserved for visible fronts/tops.
- **Poplar / basswood**: soft, light, for drawer interiors, backs, low-visibility parts.

Selection criteria: mechanical load, appearance, dimensional stability, budget, and fit with the
planned finish.

## Hardware — sizing

### Concealed hinges (cup, Ø35)
- **Cup boring**: Ø35 mm, **3–5 mm** from the front edge (cup center ≈ 21–22 mm from the edge for a
  standard Ø35 boring; follow the hinge's spec sheet).
- **Crank (overlay)**: 0 crank ≈ **full-overlay** door; half crank ≈ two doors on one side; large
  crank ≈ **inset** door. Choose by front type.
- **Number of hinges per door** (height, ~18 mm panel):

| Door height (mm) | No. of hinges |
|------------------|---------------|
| up to 900 | 2 |
| 900 – 1600 | 3 |
| 1600 – 2000 | 4 |
| 2000 – 2400 | 5 |

  Add a hinge for heavy doors (thick solid wood, large width) or tall doors.

### Drawer slides
- **Standard lengths (mm)**: 250, 300, 350, 400, 450, 500, 550 (sometimes 600).
- **Depth rule**: the usable inside depth of the carcass must be **≥ slide length**, and the carcass
  outside depth **≥ slide length + setback** (front, back, clearance) — in practice carcass ≥ slide +
  ~15–25 mm. Never pick a slide longer than the carcass allows.
- **Side clearance** (ball-bearing full-extension, standard ~12.5–13 mm per side): drawer box width =
  **inside carcass width − 26 mm** (13 mm each side). Always check the exact figure given by the
  chosen slide maker.
- Types: roller (cheap, partial extension), ball-bearing full-extension (standard), under-mount with
  soft-close (premium, require a precise drawer box).

### Shelf pins / supports
- Ø5 mm holes (32 mm system) or Ø3 mm depending on the pin; depth ~8–10 mm.
- Drill in regular rows at **32 mm centers** (32 mm system) for height adjustment.
- 4 pins per adjustable shelf.

### Screws, dowels, biscuits
- **Particleboard screws**: for an 18 mm panel, **4 × 40** (common T-joint) to **4 × 50**; pre-drill
  particleboard/melamine to avoid blow-out. In the panel edge, holding power is low → prefer
  dowels/biscuits or connectors.
- **Dowels**: Ø **8 mm**, 30–40 mm long; boring depth a bit over half into each part; regular centers
  (often 32 mm).
- **Biscuits**: sizes **#0, #10, #20** by joint thickness/length (#20 whenever the part allows);
  quick to cut with a biscuit joiner, good alignment.
- **Cam connectors** (minifix type): assembly/disassembly, ideal for flat-pack furniture.

### Misc
- **Handles / knobs**: standard centers 96, 128, 160 mm (pulls); M4 screws.
- **Feet / levelers**: adjustable feet to correct an uneven floor; toe kick set back ~50–60 mm for
  foot comfort.
- **Wall fixing**: brackets + anchors **suited to the wall** (drywall: expansion/Molly anchors or
  screwing into the studs; concrete/brick: appropriate anchors). For tall or wall-hung units, rails/
  hangers sized to the load. Secure any tall unit against tipping.

## Finishes (reminder)
- **Varnish**: hard protection, brings out the wood; several coats, sand between passes.
- **Oil**: natural look, repairable, but less protection; periodic maintenance.
- **Wax**: soft to the touch, matte; weak protection, water-sensitive.
- **Paint** (preferably on MDF): primer then 2 coats; fine sanding between coats.
- Always **sand with the grain** (80 → 120 → 180/240) and dust off before finishing. Mask and
  ventilation mandatory (dust and solvents).
